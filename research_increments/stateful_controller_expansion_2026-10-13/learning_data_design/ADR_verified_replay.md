# ADR: Objective-qualified, causally checked replay metadata

Accepted for this CPU prototype; training and new collection remain separate gates.

The input evidence is four calibration and sixteen test episodes from the frozen scripted robot timing component, plus eight already seen three-rotor development witnesses. All records retain their original roles. Robot test outcomes are primary TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN = 8/2/6, strict sampled bilateral hold = 0/16; full timed+hold success is 0/20 robot episodes. Mechanical prerequisites do not close scientific or training admission. The CPU witnesses are scoped known-model rotor results, not robot demonstrations, multiple neural experts, or student rollouts.

Two candidate boundaries were considered. A flat trajectory dataset with one success bit is easy to train from, but loses uncertainty, original roles, event identity and objective scope; it also invites pairing a post-step image with a command that already occurred. An objective-qualified episode catalogue plus a separate causal-candidate table preserves those distinctions and can later support DP, RL and student-state labels. We select the latter. The trade-off is explicit quarantine and more provenance fields; the benefit is a reviewable gate that cannot convert mechanical or primary timing success into full success implicitly.

`VerifiedReplayEpisode/0.1` is the canonical JSONL boundary. It has original role, source/audit identities, a namespaced original clock, immutable event identities, three-valued contract/objective labels, split group and objective admissions. Audited event and reward information lives only in `audit_only`/`labels`. The student-input boundary is separate, accepts plain public data only, and is not materialized in this prototype. The interface is a two-method `MetadataAdapter` Protocol; the current implementation reads hash-bound metadata and streams dispatch logs. It does not load images or SDK/native physics.

`VerifiedReplayCausalCandidate/0.1` is a separate JSONL boundary. Initial image 0 is captured before the first submission. Other images are post-step: capture index k follows integration k−1 and precedes FIFO submission k. Labels therefore reference submission k or a legal later chunk, never integration k−1 or a delayed application of a past command. At equal native timestamps the frozen source order must explicitly establish capture-before-submission. An action has its own submission identity/time, actual FIFO application identity/time, and separately unresolved physical effect. Applying a target is not physical task completion.

The current renderer exported captures, not policy image-delivery or image-conditioned decision logs. Every temporally plausible current pair is therefore UNKNOWN for causal training admission; final captures without a future submission are rejected. Public task cue delivery is also clocked: a later `public_cue.json` cannot be retroactively inserted into earlier policy observations. JPEGs are lossy and cannot reconstruct the original uncompressed RGB digest. Current `student_input` is empty, and all training flags remain false.

Record-level TRUE, FALSE and UNKNOWN are preserved. `full_timed_and_hold` is the conjunction of mechanical, primary timing and strict sampled hold; false hold cannot be omitted. Partial mechanical priors, definite objective failures, unresolved outcomes and blind redundancy can be catalogued with their own qualifications. They are not admitted as full-success DP rows. Missing/invalid episodes remain in their original denominator; multiple contracts or captures do not increase episode counts. The adapter reuses existing independent audited labels, and its receipt is metadata transformation evidence rather than a second independent physical oracle.

All current records use `LEGACY_SEEN_METADATA`. They cannot become future heldout examples. A later explicitly authorized training release may use `LEGACY_SEEN_TRAIN` while retaining original test/calibration roles; that makes the old episodes unsuitable as untouched heldout evaluation of the resulting learner. This component neither executes nor permanently forbids such future training. Episode and related-scene/duplicate groups, not frames, are the split unit. New heldout collection requires a split fixed before data collection, different recorded identities and a declared task/dynamics grouping. The twenty robot episodes share a scripted fixed-scene/NPZ-policy group; the eight known-model CPU configurations are also development seen.

The old strict-hold contract remains unchanged and unsuccessful. Its bilateral requirement through composite stable-release onset includes opening samples. Before a new complete demonstration collection, investigate joint satisfiability and distinguish support acquisition, handoff, unloading, opening and stable confirmation. A support-handoff objective may be appropriate, but it requires a new prospectively frozen contract; it cannot repair or relabel the old negative result, and no all-policy impossibility has been proved.

The public/private boundary is reviewed dataflow and a typed plain-data contract. Recursive reserved-key rejection catches explicit private poses, referee/fault/oracle labels and native getter objects; it does not detect renamed privileged numbers or provide a hostile-process sandbox. A future loader still needs channel provenance and delivered-time evidence. Privileged referee observations may supply target/reward labels to an authorized learner but never be concatenated to deployed student inputs.

Consequences: downstream loaders consume only separately admitted objective rows; new contract and dataset versions retain old records; collection captures delivered observations and action causality explicitly; student-state teachers receive a separately frozen information/budget contract. No migration of frozen datasets, old audits or public website is performed.
